How the race ended

Dowdle came within 16 seconds of missing the bell on loop 102. Five loops later, it was Noll who missed it.

Compare any of the 15 runners, loop by loop →

By the numbers

  • Mark Dowdle: 107 loops, 445.8 mi. 10 hours 32 minutes of rest banked.
  • Jon Noll: 106 loops, 441.7 mi. 10 hours 26 minutes of rest banked.

Rest banked is the time left over each hour after the loop, added up across the race.

Going into Big's, Dowdle had won every backyard ultra he ran since 2024. Seven races, seven wins. But he had never run 100 loops.

By Wednesday morning, two runners were left: Dowdle and Jon Noll. For five hours, Dowdle was the faster one.

Then came loop 100. Dowdle got back with one minute to spare. Noll had almost five.

Two loops later, it got worse. The clock read 59:44 when Dowdle crossed the line. That left him 16 seconds before the bell. No time to sit down. He turned around in the corral, caught his breath and went back out.

Was this the end of the streak?

It wasn't. Dowdle never came that close again. Two loops later, he ran 50:24, his fastest loop in six hours.

Now it was Noll who slowed. Loop 106 took him 56:07, his slowest in 14 hours. On loop 107, he did not make it back in time. Dowdle did.

After four and a half days, they had banked almost the same rest: less than seven minutes apart.

Mark Dowdle is 29. He is from St. Paul, Minnesota.

His first three backyard ultras did not end with a win. He stopped before 24 loops all three times.

Then came WausaUltra in 2024. 34 loops and his first win. The streak started there.

His 2026 so far:

  • April: Go One More, 73 loops
  • May: WausaUltra, 31 loops
  • August: Last Soul Ultra, 94 loops
  • October: Big's, 107 loops

That is 305 loops in one year. About 1,270 miles.

Last Soul ended in August. Two months later he was back on a start line, and he ran 13 more loops than he did in Germany.

Big's was also his first time past 100 loops, joining the 100-yard club. Only 21 other runners in the world have gone past 100. Phil Gore, Sam Harvey and Łukasz Wróbel are among them.

His 107 is a new personal best. It ties him for 16th on the world list, with Ihor Verys of Canada.

How a backyard ultra works

Every runner starts a 4.2 mi loop at the top of each hour. Finish it inside the hour and the time left over is your rest. Miss the next start and your race is over. The last runner to finish a loop alone wins.
